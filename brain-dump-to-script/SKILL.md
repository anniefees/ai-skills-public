# Brain dump to script

Turn a raw brain dump into one polished script that sounds like the user on a good day. Organize and tighten without rewriting them into someone else.

## Inputs and context
- Required: the brain dump, including rambling notes, transcribed speech or bullet fragments.
- Series or format: use what the user supplies, such as talking head, storytime, POV, list or day-in-the-life. Infer from recent context or an existing draft for this piece before asking.
- Optional: target length, platform, requested hook, reference script.
- Check the conversation, memory, current voice guidance and approved series conventions first. Read a relevant existing draft when available, without changing it.
- Ask one focused question only if a missing detail would materially change the script. Otherwise draft immediately. Note a material assumption briefly, not a lengthy intake.

## Drafting
1. Find the central point: what is this piece actually about? Keep supporting details, cut tangents, and do not split it into separate pieces unless asked.
2. Preserve the user's distinctive phrases, slang, jokes, stance and specific details. Smooth grammar only as much as speaking aloud needs.
3. Organize the ideas so the audience can follow without knowing the brain dump. Supply connective language, not invented context. Establish necessary context, what happened or the cause, its effect or realization, then a payoff. Adapt to the actual format rather than forcing this order onto every script.
4. Trim repetition, false starts and filler. Keep enough detail for the story to make sense, not just the shortest version.
5. Open with a natural hook based on an interesting true detail. It must pay off in the script. The hook is part of the script, not a menu of alternatives.
6. Use natural spoken transitions rather than essay phrasing.
7. End on the payoff or a natural closing thought. Add a call to action only when requested or part of an approved series convention.
8. Match the series structure, recurring lines, intended length and medium. For text-led formats, produce the actual on-screen sequence rather than inventing a voiceover. Add visual cues only when requested or essential to understanding the format.

## Guardrails
- Never invent experiences, results, numbers, quotes, dialogue or factual details. Trace specifics to the dump or verified context. If essential information is missing, ask or use a clearly marked short [placeholder].
- Verify uncertain external factual or technical claims when needed before retaining them. Do not turn the user's subjective experience into an unsupported universal claim.
- Do not change their stance or turn documentation into advice or coaching.
- Follow current user voice guidance over generic creator formulas. Avoid slogans, catchphrases and hype they would not say.
- Do not automatically add separate hook options, post captions, hashtags, shot lists or explanations of your edits.
- Do not save to a notes tool or elsewhere, schedule, message teammates or post unless separately requested. Keep this a writing task.

## Default delivery
Return one clean script in chat, ready to say aloud, in short spoken paragraphs or lines. No title or headers unless the format needs segments. An optional one-line assumption or approximate spoken duration is fine if helpful. Duration is an estimate, not measured runtime. No menus or alternatives unless requested.

## Revisions
Start from the latest draft and the user's latest feedback. Change only what the feedback targets, preserving unaffected wording unless adjacent changes are necessary for flow. Do not restart from the raw dump or silently revert approved wording. Apply current user instructions over older preferences while respecting factual accuracy. If a newly expressed preference should persist, use the assistant's normal memory guidance rather than putting personal details into this generic skill.

## Final checks
- Is there a clear central point and an understandable progression?
- Does it sound like the user, with their own good phrases preserved?
- Can every factual detail be traced to the dump or verified context?
- Are repetition and irrelevant tangents removed without losing essential meaning?
- Does it read naturally aloud, with a hook the piece pays off and an ending that lands?
- Does it follow the format, series conventions and current voice and punctuation rules?
- Is it free of forced CTAs, coaching pivots and unrequested extras?
- Have you avoided saving, publishing and other side tasks?
