# Video editing playbook

**Draft. Real rendered-export validation is pending.** This is a reusable editing procedure. It is not a fixed production workflow. Finishing an edit does not approve publishing, and this file reports no export test result.

## Goal

Edit for trust and clarity. A viewer should be able to follow the point, and the footage should support what the piece shows. Views, reach, and virality are outside this procedure.

This is a generic procedure, not a personal style preset. The account's native kit stays in a separate user-controlled specification.

## What you need before you start

Collect these from the user. Leave a material gap as a blocker and ask. Do not invent a source, a claim, or a kit to fill it.

- The current brief, audience, and intent
- The authorized source version
- Format, platform, and aspect ratio
- Any user-approved take default
- The account style specification and its revision: native title preset, caption treatment, filter, layout, cover template, authorized audio, and dated overrides
- Reference examples, audio instructions, feedback, and the current approval state, when the user supplied them

No vendor API or paid account is required. Use the editor, storage, and playback setup the user already has.

## Authority

Apply the latest applicable direct user instruction first. Scope each decision to this account, series, output, and revision.

1. Latest direct instruction for this output.
2. User-approved take default, then other approved series conventions.
3. Current account style specification.
4. This playbook.

Account style rules outrank a look inferred from reference videos. Approved series conventions outrank generic defaults. Historical examples, draft notes, and analyst suggestions are not standing instructions.

Ask about a material unresolved conflict. Do not guess. Never borrow another account's kit.

## Workflow

Run these seven stages in order. Use the edit plan, output manifest, and review notes templates at the end of this skill. A later stage can send the work back to an earlier one. After any change to the cut, retest the new rendered export before you change state.

### 1. Brief, audience, and intent

Write the job down before cutting.

- **Audience.** Who the piece is for.
- **Intent.** What the viewer should understand, feel, or be able to do afterward.
- **Promise.** The claim the available footage can support.
- **Format.** Platform, aspect ratio, and any runtime guidance already in the brief. Do not copy a short-form duration onto a piece that was not briefed that way.
- **Sources.** Identity, version, and what is authorized.
- **Take default.** The user-approved rule, or `none recorded`.
- **Locked work.** Approved or hand-edited sections to leave as they are.
- **Rights.** Which pictures, music, and effects are authorized.

Open one output manifest for this version. Record the account, format, source identity and version, output version, each direct override and its provenance, authorized audio, style-specification revision, and approval state. Keep every original, project file, and export. Confirm that no other editor owns the work.

Stage result: edit-plan header filled in, new manifest opened, material blockers written down.

### 2. Hook, story, and payoff beat plan

Plan the beats before assembling the picture.

- **Hook.** Use the authorized hook wording at the start. A title can carry the hook before the spoken line. Do not hold the title by delaying speech, and do not promise a payoff the footage does not deliver.
- **Story.** List the context, steps, or turns the intent needs. Keep the chronology the brief intends. An intentional reorder needs a meaning-preserving reason on the edit plan.
- **Payoff.** End on the result, answer, or landing the sources contain. Do not write an ending the footage does not have.

For each beat, record its purpose, source range, and carrier: speech, visual, or music.

Read the plan against the intent. Someone who has not seen the footage should be able to say what the piece is about and what it must not claim. That check is for trust and clarity.

Stage result: a beat table on the edit plan.

### 3. Source review and meaning-preserving speech assembly

Review every available source before you commit to a cut. When the piece has little or no speech, still review the full source and preserve the meaning of on-screen words and of the action. Mark speech-only steps as not applicable on the edit plan.

1. Treat a transcript or automatic speech recognition as a set of candidate cuts. Set word boundaries by listening. The transcript does not establish a safe cut.
2. Compare complete takes for meaning, delivery, gestures, and natural cadence. Apply a verified user-approved take default when one is recorded. This playbook does not add its own ordinal default. Do not pick a take because it is second or later unless that is the approved default, or the comparison itself supports the choice. Record the choice and the reason.
3. Assemble speech so the retained words mean what the speaker meant. Remove unhelpful dead air, accidental repetition, real stumbles, false starts, and excess breaths where the piece gets clearer. Keep intentional pauses, natural breaths, emphasis, emotion, and gestures. This playbook sets no silence threshold, breath threshold, fixed runtime, or zoom quota.
4. Keep complete words, syllables, and consonants. Leave speech speed and pitch as recorded. Do not fabricate speech or an experience, and do not splice words into a different meaning.
5. Keep a source-range edit list with a reason for every cut. Check order, reused handles, replay, and accidental overlap. When audio sounds doubled, separate a reflection that was already in the source from a replay the edit created.
6. Listen across every join with context on both sides. If a join fails, keep the uncut original until a source-supported correction exists, then retest that join. A meter reading does not overrule clipped speech or an unnatural cadence.

Stage result: an edit list on the edit plan. Each cut has a reason. Each join is marked listened-to or still open.

### 4. External brand binding

Bind the look, captions, cover, and required sound from the current account style specification. That specification is external to this skill. Do not invent fonts, colors, title layouts, filters, cover marks, or music from this file, from a reference video, or from another account.

Bind each item the specification provides:

- Native title preset
- Caption treatment
- Filter
- Layout
- Cover template
- Authorized audio
- Dated overrides that apply to this output

Duplicate the approved cover template. Leave the master unchanged. A reference can inform pacing. It cannot donate its fonts, colors, or music.

List every missing native component on the manifest, individually. While a required component is missing, the state is provisional. Do not label that output brand-complete.

Stage result: manifest fields for the style-spec revision, components bound, and components missing.

### 5. Captions, contextual visuals, purposeful zoom, SFX, and audio

Build picture and sound from the beat plan and the bound kit.

**Captions.** Proofread against the exact retained speech. The planned script is not the source of truth. Flag an unclear word instead of inventing a spelling. After the last cut, remap every caption through the final edit list. Follow the bound caption treatment for readable duration, contrast, line breaks, and placement away from faces and platform controls. Keep the hook, captions, and other graphics from occupying the same place at the same time.

**Contextual visuals.** Where a beat needs a place, object, or piece of evidence, use footage, graphics, or animation the user is allowed to use. Prefer the creator's own suitable footage. Label material from elsewhere, and do not present it as a personal event that did not happen. Leave a beat without a visual when no suitable source exists.

**Purposeful zoom and effects.** Use a direct cut when the content changes. An in-shot punch-in or emphasis graphic is for clarity, emphasis, or humor the footage supports. That is a different choice from a decorative transition. Apply only effects the brief or the style specification permits. Do not run a repeating zoom or a fixed effect cadence that no beat requires.

**SFX.** Add a sound effect when it marks a real beat, such as a reveal or a cut to evidence. Do not bed the timeline in constant effects.

**Audio.** Keep the voice clear. Keep original audio when it carries place, reaction, or proof. Add music only when that recording is authorized. If the brief names a recording, that recording is required. Missing requested audio blocks the export. It is not permission to substitute. Place the required sound in the export, with rights and timing checked, before any upload discussion. A library listing does not by itself establish usage rights. Do not swap a song silently. Leave performance speed as recorded unless a speed treatment is separately authorized. Align music-driven cuts to the actual recording's beats. Do not assume a tempo.

**Cover.** Deliver a separate cover image made from the duplicated template. When the brief requires a baked cover frame, use a single first frame and do not shift speech, captions, or music to make room. Check the rendered export against the platform crop. Do not invent a series label or an episode number.

Stage result: the caption map matches the final edit list. Each visual, zoom, effect, sound effect, and music cue is tied to a beat and an authorization, or it is omitted on purpose.

### 6. Full export and independent review

Export the delivery file with the tools already available. Default delivery, unless the brief requires another format, is H.264 video and AAC audio at a source-appropriate frame rate and the requested aspect ratio.

Record technical checks separately from perceptual review. Technical checks confirm that the file plays, the dimensions match the request, audio is present, and picture and sound stay in sync.

Set the state from evidence on this version:

- `timing candidate` — the assembly exists, and full export review is not done.
- `provisional` — review happened, and one or more required native components are still missing.
- `native verified` — the intended native assets were inspected in the rendered result.
- `user approved` — the user explicitly approved this version.

A completion marker, a successful decode, or an upload does not set the state. A candidate that fails review stays rejected, including when the file plays.

Independent review of the rendered export is mandatory before any claim that the edit passed:

- The reviewer is separate from the step that generated the cut.
- The reviewer watches the full exported video.
- The reviewer listens to the full exported audio on headphones.
- The reviewer checks every join.

Record who or what reviewed it, and record any limit, such as a section that would not play, a missing headphone pass, or a reviewer who also generated the cut. File metadata, duration, frame count, a transcript, a waveform, and still frames do not substitute for watching and listening.

An optional external review tool may add a supplemental pass. Name the tool and what it covered. The tool does not replace the full watch and listen. If the full review cannot be done, write that the review is missing and do not claim the export passed.

On the actual export, confirm:

- The hook is legible at the start, in the bound brand treatment.
- The intended takes are complete, with syllables and meaning intact.
- Unhelpful dead air and excess breaths are gone, and intentional pauses and personality remain.
- Zooms and effects serve a beat.
- Captions match the retained words, stay in sync, are proofread, sit clear of faces and controls, and do not collide with other graphics.
- Contextual visuals appear where they help and a permitted source exists.
- The voice is clear and consistent, and the only music or effects present are authorized.
- The ending is one the sources support.

Editing does not authorize a production upload, a share, a message, scheduling, or a workflow-status change.

Stage result: export file recorded on the manifest, technical notes separate from review notes, state set from the evidence above.

### 7. Revision and retest

Keep returned or rejected revisions in their own queue, apart from new edits. Start from the latest authorized version. Change the parts the user requested. Leave the rest of the approved work as it is. Do not overwrite a hand-edited or approved project.

A correction, a new export, or a later approval gets a new manifest that links to the previous one. Do not edit an old manifest so that a changed input or a changed result looks like the original record.

Listen again to every join the revision touched, with context on both sides. Then repeat stage 6 on the new export. A timeline fix stays unaccepted until that independent review of the new rendered picture and sound is recorded. Set the new state from the new evidence.

Publishing, scheduling, and approval stay separate and need their own authorization.

Stage result: new manifest, new export, new review notes, prior version left intact.

## Adjust the beat plan by video type

Use the same seven stages. Change the beats to fit the type.

### Talking head and personal story

Keep the speaker primary. Let complete phrases land. Show context, progression, and a genuine ending. Use short readable caption groups from the current style specification, relevant cutaways, and selective emphasis. Do not impose a fixed shot-change interval. Do not recast a story as coaching.

### Tutorial and educational explanation

Show the demonstration or the reference when the speech mentions it. Protect private information in screen recordings. Add a short definition when the audience needs it. If the demonstration footage is missing, say so. Do not fabricate an interface, a demonstration, or a claim.

### Vlog, getting-ready, and day-in-the-life

Keep the chronology and the story the brief intends. Use atmosphere and natural sound when they carry the place or the action. Keep the action understandable. Protect reactions that carry meaning. Do not build a narrative event out of unrelated footage.

### Text-led footage and low-effort short

Use authorized on-screen wording and relevant action. Leave enough time to read the text. Keep the hook and the payoff understandable. Choose audio only within the permission in the brief.

### Music-led montage and trending-audio format

Use the authorized recording the brief names. Align meaningful cuts to that recording's actual beats. Keep relevant source sound unless its removal is authorized. Missing requested audio blocks the export.

### Dance, sport, and other performance footage

Keep the important action visible. When the piece needs dance text, center it without covering the performer or the essential movement. For a music-driven piece, align key motion and cuts to real beats. Leave the performance speed unchanged unless a speed treatment is separately authorized.

### Build, challenge, and recurring series

Follow the approved series conventions and the designated quality reference. Do not import that reference's unverified fonts, colors, or music. Keep the setup, process, context, and payoff the episode needs. State facts the sources support. Do not invent an episode number, a permanent stamp, or a call to action.

### Long form and other aspect ratios

Follow the requested platform and the source. Do not force short-form duration, framing, or caption density onto another format. Ask only when a material requirement is missing.

## Dated lessons

When a job produces a reusable craft lesson, record the decision date, the format, the generic lesson, the evidence type, and whether it is approved. Keep personal source details in the user's private account record, outside this skill. An observation or a failed test is not yet a rule. Propose a change for approval. Do not rewrite this skill during an edit, and do not turn a performance anecdote into a rule.

The three watch-and-listen lines have to be `yes` before the export can pass. If any line is `no`, the review is incomplete. Do not mark the export passed. Stills, waveforms, transcripts, and file metadata do not change a `no` into a `yes`.

## Done when

- The edit plan, the caption map, and the final cut describe the same version.
- This output has its own manifest, and older manifests were left as written.
- Every missing native component is listed, and the state matches the evidence.
- Review notes cover a full independent watch and listen of the rendered export, or the missing review is disclosed and no pass is claimed.
- A revision was retested on its new export, including the joins that changed.
- Publishing, scheduling, and user approval remain separate actions.
